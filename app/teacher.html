Open the forest

One change, two possible stories

Draft 15–20 minute activity for middle-school ecosystem reasoning. This is a pilot-ready guide, not an evaluated curriculum. A general science/biology teacher, facilitator or learner can help test usability; an ecology specialist is not required for the first check.

The short Guided investigation offers a replayable instructional example. For the longer learner-led activity below, use Ask “what if?”. The two simulations still run together, but the learner sees one forest by default and can switch or compare both. Exact values are under See measurements and event records.

Objective and setup

Learners predict an indirect effect, change one model input, compare a baseline, cite evidence and name a model limitation. No student accounts or live AI are required. Open the forest, choose Ask “what if?” and select What if it rains less?. Start from the current forest to retain a personal creature; reset beforehand only if you want the standard seed-7 start. The model measures are simulated indices, not observations of a real location.

Facilitation sequence

  1. Before/predict · 3 minutes. Optional before-use question: “Assume algae feed insect larvae, which feed fish. How could reduced algae growth indirectly affect fish?” Collect the response without teaching the chain. Ask which forest measure will change first under less rain and why.
  2. Experiment · 5 minutes. Read the exact proposed rainfall change aloud. Confirm two worlds copy the same state. Advance 13 weeks, then 52. Ask what stayed fixed and what changed. A model week advances the rules; it is not a real-time observation.
  3. Inspect/explain · 5 minutes. Look at the resource/population table and event references. Collect the learner's explanation before showing the local evidence guide. Distinguish the recorded difference from a possible causal mechanism. Inspect ten random continuations if time permits: ranges describe model variation, not confidence about nature.
  4. Transfer/discuss · 3–5 minutes. Ask: “Assume grass feeds grasshoppers, which feed small birds. How could reduced grass growth indirectly affect birds?” Ask what the model leaves out and what outdoor evidence would be needed. Save a discovery card with the learner's permission.

Optional extension · everyday connections and our tool

Allow another 3–5 minutes after collecting the transfer response. These discussion prompts are separate from the 0–2 reasoning rubric; there is no required personal action or preferred opinion.

  1. Connect to a familiar place. “Think of a plant or animal near home or school. What does it depend on? What would you need to observe before deciding how to help it?” Distinguish a model-inspired question from evidence about that place.
  2. Examine our own tool. “This activity uses a computer, and optional AI features use more computing. Which features helped you investigate? Could saved artwork, prepared questions or captions serve the same purpose?” Keep narration or dictation when needed for participation; learners need not justify an access need.
  3. Identify what we do not know. “What would we need to measure before claiming this tool helps the environment overall?” Useful information includes actual learning/use, electricity, hardware and provider infrastructure. API price and token counts alone do not establish carbon or water use.

Microverse’s simulation, prepared questions and local evidence guide run without AI inference. Optional image generation, custom Spark requests and transcription use provider inference. Natural narration uses a local neural model: local does not mean impact-free. Saved generated art still has a production footprint. Recent successful results are reused within bounded server-session caches; restart or eviction can require another call.

AI has environmental costs, including electricity demand and water use associated with data-centre cooling and power generation. See IEA: Energy and AI and Berkeley Lab’s data-centre energy report. These describe infrastructure, not Microverse-specific measurements. We have not measured this project’s footprint or established a net environmental benefit.

Reasoning rubric · exploratory only

Use the same 0–2 guide on the two hypothetical food-chain responses: 0 unsupported/no relevant link; 1 describes a direct resource or food effect; 2 links the resource through the intermediate organism to the consumer. Record missing responses separately. Do not treat a small, uncontrolled trial as proof of learning gains, causal efficacy or long-term transfer.

Model boundaries

Rainfall affects grass and browse growth under simplified, tuned rules. Rabbits graze; deer browse; foxes/wolves hunt specified prey. Scarce species may immigrate unless excluded by removal. Companions receive off-map care. Temperature, pollution, disease and real habitat suitability are not modelled. Images are illustrations; no model result establishes real-world ecological validity. Paired random streams can diverge when interventions change draw counts.

The worksheet contains responses and facilitator notes but no pictures. Its model-only rerun link removes pictures, creature names and learner notes. The separate full replay may include personal creature images; save/share it deliberately.

Pilot status

No teacher/learner sessions have been recorded yet. Target one short usability trial first; expand toward two facilitators and roughly five learners if available. If recruitment is unavailable, publish the protocol and retain “untested” status. Do not fabricate testimonials or outcomes.
