BEHIND THIS LITTLE WORLD

A little AI.
A lot of reusable craft.

A photograph becomes a painting. A painting becomes a character. Then a small set of rules gives us a world to question.

01 / A familiar face

A new look.
The same little someone.

Muse Image uses a photo as a reference for a watercolor character. SAM separates the character from its background.

The result needs a human check: markings, accessories and missing limbs can go wrong. The player reviews it before welcoming it in.

See the real photo example ↗
02 / A few tiny footsteps

Made once.
Moved many times.

Local code cuts and moves the image. The entrance reuses cached poses, so an ordinary step or scroll does not need a new image request.

A cutout does not tell us an animal’s anatomy. Movement is an artistic interpretation, and simple whole-image motion remains an option when the rig looks wrong.

03 / A world you can question

What changed?
Follow the record.

The ecosystem runs in code: plants grow, animals feed, and events are recorded. The same saved state and experiment can be replayed.

Prepared questions work without a model call. Optional Spark turns custom wording into a supported proposal or selects evidence. It does not directly rewrite the rules or invent the numbers shown.

These are simplified model outcomes, not outdoor forecasts. We have not yet measured classroom learning gains.

AN HONEST FOOTPRINT

Even this little world
uses real resources.

AI generation, local computation, downloads and devices all use resources. Reusing artwork and running ordinary simulation steps locally avoids repeated model calls for those actions. It does not erase the cost of creating the artwork or prove an overall environmental benefit.

We have not measured this project’s total energy, carbon or water use, or established that one model provider is greener for our workload.
